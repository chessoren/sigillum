The problem

Everyone says “sovereign”.
Nobody measures it.

Unverifiable promises

A public buyer cannot tell a sovereign tool from one that only says so.

Hidden dependencies

An app on an open model can still send data to a foreign API, an analytics service or a CDN — or stop when the connection drops.

No common yardstick

Two teams that say “sovereign” do not mean the same thing. Organisers cannot tell which projects to showcase.

SigillumHack Apertus 2026 · Track 2B04
The method

Three rules make the grade credible.

1

The proof rule

A claim without a trace never earns more than C. Missing information is shown as unknown — and caps the grade at C.

2

The weakest link

The final grade is the worst of the five answers. Perfect on four but data goes abroad? Not an A.

3

A dated, tamper-evident seal

Date, commit, method version and a SHA-256 fingerprint. It expires after 90 days: a project that changes must be examined again.

SigillumHack Apertus 2026 · Track 2B05
Apertus v1.5 8B · local

Apertus reads, reconciles and explains. The evidence decides.

The grade never depends on the model. Apertus does what fixed rules cannot — and every answer is checked before use.

1

Reads

Extracts what the documentation claims about sovereignty. A quote must exist word for word.

2

Reconciles

Sets each claim against the code traces and names the gap. It must cite evidence IDs that exist.

3

Explains

Writes — or confirms — the verdict in plain German, French, Italian and Romansh. A line in the wrong language is rejected.

Per project: up to 7 Apertus calls (read 1 · reconcile 1 · explain 5) · about 5 minutes on 4 CPU cores, no GPU · every rejected answer is logged as feedback for the Apertus team
